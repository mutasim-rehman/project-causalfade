### Cohort 016 result

Both 768-response arms completed with strict-valid JSON throughout and identical frozen schedules. The prespecified Q4_K_M conflict order effect replicated on 96 fresh families: historical-first accuracy was 175/192 (91.1%) versus 55/192 (28.6%) randomized-first. The family-mean difference was +62.5 percentage points (95% t interval +56.6 to +68.4; t(95)=21.10, p=1.24×10⁻³⁷; bootstrap interval +56.8 to +68.2). Both formats also changed sharply on aligned items: randomized-first accuracy was 192/192 for each, while historical-first was 96/192 for F16 and 105/192 for Q4_K_M. F16 activated on all 192 aligned historical-first prompts; Q4_K_M activated on 153/192. Thus the source-order sensitivity extends beyond conflict handling and appears tied to action behavior. The primary order effect is replicated for this setup, while its mechanism and generality remain open.

# Cohort 025 — retrospective methods screen of cohorts 001–024

**Classification:** post-outcome exploratory analysis of existing files. No LLM request, new SCM family, model selection, or confirmatory experiment was run. The 24 prior cohort directories and 59 run result files are inventoried in the [machine-readable analysis](archive_methods_screen.json). Fifty-eight runs have success status and contain 40,032 persisted raw attempts; the one interrupted cohort 008 attempt has 26 raw attempts and 64 scored schedule rows, including placeholders. It is retained in the audit but excluded from completed-run interpretation. The [analysis code](../../tools/analyze_cohort025_archive.py) leaves all earlier cohort files unchanged and records raw and result SHA-256 for each run. For cohorts 001–014, stored strict-parser validity can differ from the separately documented historical fence-tolerant reading; cohort 025 does not retroactively rescore those runs.

## Retrospective questions and results

**Metamorphic response checks.** Cohort 024 provides within-family pairs that change answer-option order, randomized-study A/B name, evidence order, or semantic versus neutral response contract while preserving the relevant correct action. On Mistral F16's semantic causal decision pairs, only **6/128** retained the same answer after answer-option reversal. Its source-selection pairs retained the same answer in **0/128** cases; because the governing source was unchanged, these flips follow displayed option position. The prior cohort 024 audit independently found first-option selection on **250/256** semantic causal decisions and **256/256** source-selection decisions. This is a strong task-specific position policy, not evidence that the model used source identity. Qwen F16 kept the same semantic causal answer in 111/128 option-order pairs, but only 47/128 pairs had both answers correct. Robustness and correctness are separate endpoints.

**Information and response bias.** On cohort 024 Qwen F16 semantic causal decisions, the empirical scheduled-response information about the true intervention sign was **0.031 bits**, while conditional information about the observational-association sign given truth was **0.084 bits**. On matched semantic noncausal controls, the corresponding values were **0.230** and **0.052 bits**. The response alphabet includes `invalid` rather than silently converting it to decline. These plug-in information estimates have finite-sample bias, no multiplicity adjustment and only eight independent SCM families. They describe behavior under this balanced fixture; they do not identify the model's internal evidence weights or show causal specificity. Qwen Q4_K_M's neutral-contract causal decisions were valid but **256/256 `X=1`**, yielding zero measured response information about either sign. F16 was already strongly activation-biased under that contract.

The archive also reports core-decision hit and false-alarm counts with corrected descriptive `d_prime` and criterion for all **58 successful runs**, using each cohort's stored strict decoder. For example, cohort 018 F16 had 37/64 beneficial hits and 37/64 harmful false alarms (`d_prime` 0.00), while cohort 024 Qwen F16 semantic decisions had 104/128 hits and 121/128 false alarms (`d_prime` −0.69). These figures are not exchangeable across prompt designs. Cohort 001 F16 has zero valid responses under its *stored strict parser*, so its coordinates are undefined; the historical fence-tolerant analysis remains a separate documented interpretation, not a zero-capability claim.

**Paired format transitions.** On identical cohort 024 semantic causal items, Qwen Q4_K_M gained 6 correct answers and lost 7 relative to F16 (**−0.4 percentage points** net). Its semantic noncausal scorecards had 5 gains and 15 losses. Llama Q4_K_M's semantic causal items had 3 gains and 4 losses, but its semantic noncausal controls had 198 losses and zero gains: all 256 Q4 control responses were invalid under the 64-token cap. These transition matrices expose the individual-item changes hidden by aggregate scores, while preserving validity failures. They do not imply that gains and losses are stochastic jitter or a neural mechanism.

**Precision × evidence-order interaction.** The historical-first minus randomized-first conflict-accuracy gap was 17.2 points at F16 and 62.5 at Q4_K_M in cohort 016; the Q4-minus-F16 interaction was **+45.3 points**, as previously reported descriptively for this secondary comparison. In cohort 024's Qwen semantic conflict scaffold, both F16 and Q4 scored **13/64 historical-first and 32/64 randomized-first**, so the corresponding interaction was **0 points**. Cohort 017's Q4-minus-Q8 interaction was +4.2 points on reused cohort 016 families; Q8 is not an F16 reference. These distinct task scaffolds and model configurations cannot be pooled to assert a general “decanalization” effect. Cohort 016's prospectively frozen primary result remains valid for its own prompt population.

**Existing hint probe.** In cohort 021, ordinary and source-guided two-source decisions each scored **58/128**. Pairing by stem reveals **11 gains and 11 losses** under source guidance. A neutral guidance condition scored 56/128. This was a prompt manipulation, not the full source × calculation rescue factorial with matched placebo length and noncausal controls. It gives no identified goal-neglect or stage-specific mechanism.

**Two-point strength pilot.** Cohort 018 crossed causal directions and association signs at causal effect magnitudes 0.04 and 0.12, while the observational magnitude stayed fixed. Its cell counts are in the analysis JSON. Two causal-strength levels cannot identify a psychometric threshold, four-parameter lapse curve, or a two-dimensional causal/observational response surface.

## Method applicability boundary

The analysis JSON records an explicit status for each proposed method. The archives support descriptive signal-detection/activation measures, item transitions, presentation invariance checks, information dependence, component/control audits and limited hint/strength contrasts. They contain **no observationally identical different-graph intervention pairs**, target-population switches, full graded association grid, validated IRT anchor scale, clean strict-valid double-pass block, token logits, internal activations, confidence task, or validated cognitive reaction times. Those analyses cannot be recovered from the logs. A trial-level mixed model could fit some response probabilities, but it would not make heteroskedastic latent SDT scales or psychological routes identifiable from these binary outputs.

## Scientific decision

Cohort 025 strengthens the measurement diagnosis: option position, response contract and output validity can dominate apparent causal or precision effects. The Mistral pair result is a particularly clear demonstration within this assay, but answer-order bias is already known in LLM evaluation. The reanalysis creates **no independent replication** and no new causal-specific quantization finding. Its many selected contrasts are post-outcome and descriptive. Do not label cohort 025 as a fresh experimental cohort in a manuscript.

For a new experiment, prioritize the proposed observationally identical graph-switch assay, with both answer polarities, no-change graph controls, arithmetic and graph-comprehension probes, and a prespecified family-level primary endpoint. Use untouched SCM families for confirmation. Graded-evidence psychophysics and factorial rescue are separate later studies.
